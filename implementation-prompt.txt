Build an interactive educational website titled “From Pressure to Flow.”

Purpose:
Teach the relationship between pressure forces, shear stress, Newtonian viscosity, velocity gradients, and volumetric flow rate through computation and visualization.

Assumptions:
Steady, fully developed laminar flow of an incompressible Newtonian fluid in a horizontal, rigid circular pipe, with constant properties and no slip. Define L as the length of the fully developed section and ΔP as the pressure drop across that section. Initially support nonnegative driving inputs.

Teaching sequence:
1. Draw a cylindrical fluid element of radius r inside a pipe of radius R.
2. Balance pressure force ΔPπr² against opposing shear force τ(r)2πrL.
3. Derive shear-stress magnitude τ(r)=ΔPr/(2L).
4. Connect it to Newtonian viscosity: τ(r)=−μ du/dr for positive flow.
5. Integrate using u(R)=0 to obtain u(r)=ΔP(R²−r²)/(4μL).
6. Integrate over annular area dA=2πr dr to obtain Q=πR⁴ΔP/(8μL).

Show units directly beneath variables in the guided equations. Explain why each equation is dimensionally consistent.

Interactive explorer:
- Start with pressure drop as the only adjustable parameter.
- Progressively unlock length, viscosity, and radius through guided experiments.
- Keep velocity and shear-stress-magnitude plots visible together, with matching position axes.
- Include a linked marker showing local velocity, velocity gradient, and shear-stress magnitude.
- Provide a baseline overlay or locked-scale comparison so changes remain visually apparent.
- Add separate “Hold pressure drop fixed” and “Hold flow rate fixed” modes. The dependent quantity must be a labeled calculated readout.
- Display Q, ΔP, wall shear stress, and numerical pressure and wall shear forces with units.
- Calculate internally in SI units. Clearly identify any display-unit conversions.
- Include numeric inputs alongside sliders and a reset button.
- Support desktop and mobile layouts.
- Explain that changing inputs displays a new steady state, not a transient simulation.

Optional final section:
Introduce Reynolds number and entrance development only after the core lesson. Keep density controls and related calculations out of the main explorer.

Validation:
- Require positive radius, length, and viscosity.
- Handle zero driving input.
- Verify no slip, zero centerline velocity gradient, maximum shear-stress magnitude at the wall, and umax=2uaverage.
- Compare numerical integration of the velocity profile with analytical Q.
- Check pressure force against total wall shear force using a numerical tolerance.
- Verify expected pressure, length, viscosity, and radius scaling.
